The park

A memorial you can walk through

Theodore Roosevelt Island is an 88.5-acre wooded island in the Potomac River, between Georgetown in Washington, DC and Rosslyn, Virginia. The National Park Service manages it as a memorial to the 26th president, who is remembered for protecting public lands and wildlife.

Most presidents are remembered with a building. Roosevelt is remembered with a forest. There are no roads and no cars on the island, only footpaths through the woods, boardwalks over the marsh, and a memorial plaza in a clearing near the middle.

The memorial plaza · 1967

Four granite tablets

Around Paul Manship's statue of Roosevelt stand four 21-foot granite tablets, each carved with his words on one theme. Here is one line from each.

  1. I

    Nature

    “There is delight in the hardy life of the open.”

  2. II

    Manhood

    “It is hard to fail, but it is worse never to have tried to succeed.”

  3. III

    Youth

    “Courage, hard work, self-mastery, and intelligent effort are all essential to a successful life.”

  4. IV

    The State

    “Order without liberty and liberty without order are equally destructive.”

Timeline

From Analostan Island to a national memorial

  1. Early

    The island's old name, Analostan, is thought to come from the Nacotchtank (Anacostan) people who lived along the Potomac.

  2. 1790s

    John Mason, son of George Mason, builds a house and gardens here. People call it Mason's Island.

  3. 1863

    During the Civil War, Union soldiers, including the 1st U.S. Colored Infantry, train on the island.

  4. 1923

    The Key Bridge opens just upstream, linking Rosslyn to Georgetown.

  5. 1931

    The Theodore Roosevelt Memorial Association buys the island and gives it to the nation as a memorial.

  6. 1930s

    Following a plan by the Olmsted Brothers, Civilian Conservation Corps crews clear old fields and plant a new forest.

  7. 1964

    The Theodore Roosevelt Bridge opens across the island's southern tip.

  8. 1967

    The memorial plaza, designed by Eric Gugler with a statue by Paul Manship, is dedicated on October 27, Roosevelt's birthday.

Getting there

Visit the island

On foot or by Metro

From Rosslyn station (Blue, Orange and Silver lines), walk about 15 minutes to the footbridge on the Virginia shore.

By bike or car

The Mount Vernon Trail passes the entrance. Bikes aren't allowed on the island, so lock up at the racks. The small parking lot can only be reached from the northbound George Washington Memorial Parkway.

On the island

Walk the Swamp Trail boardwalk loop, the Upland Trail or the short Woods Trail to the memorial. Hours can change, so check the National Park Service website before you go.
